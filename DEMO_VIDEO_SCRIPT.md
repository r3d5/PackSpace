# PackSpace Demo Video Script

Target length: **2 minutes 20 seconds**  
Format: Lens Studio Preview screen recording + companion website recording + ElevenLabs voice-over.

## Recording checklist

- Record at 1080p or higher with the cursor hidden when possible.
- Begin from a clean PackSpace main menu.
- Keep each interaction deliberate and leave short pauses for the voice-over.
- Show at least two category areas placed at visibly different heights.
- Capture one item being marked complete and the HUD updating.
- Use a real six-character code during the website/import segment.
- End on the spatial workspace, not on a settings or editor screen.

## Shot list and voice-over

### 0:00–0:10 — Title and problem

**Video:** Open on the PackSpace main menu. Slowly move the preview camera to establish that the menu follows the user.

**Voice-over:**

> This is PackSpace, a spatial packing assistant for Snap Specs. Packing is physical and hands-on, so a checklist works better when it understands the room around you.

### 0:10–0:25 — Start a preset

**Video:** Select the Two-Day Work Trip preset. Show the first placement instruction.

**Voice-over:**

> I can start with a ready-made trip preset or create my own. PackSpace asks me to place each category one at a time, directly where I plan to gather those objects.

### 0:25–0:48 — Spatial placement on different surfaces

**Video:** Pinch-drag Clothes on a sofa or chair. Place Tech on a table at a different height. Place Essentials on another horizontal surface. Pause after every release so the completed area is visible.

**Voice-over:**

> CLAD helped me integrate continuous World Query surface detection and pinch-drag placement. Every completed category appears immediately, and each area keeps its own detected position and height. One zone can sit on a table while another lives on a chair, sofa, or floor.

### 0:48–1:05 — Packing interaction

**Video:** Show the category title and item list. Mark two items as collected. Frame the head-locked checklist updating from zero to two completed items.

**Voice-over:**

> The outlined zones stay in physical space while a compact checklist remains visible in the glasses display. After I bring an item to its area, I mark it collected, and both the spatial list and overall progress update without rebuilding or jittering the interface.

### 1:05–1:22 — Custom preset creation

**Video:** Return to the main menu. Select Create Custom Preset, enter a preset name and category name, place one category, and show the plus action used to add an item.

**Voice-over:**

> PackSpace is not limited to a fixed template. I can name a custom preset, add my own categories and items, and place each category anywhere on a valid horizontal surface.

### 1:22–1:38 — Persistence and resume

**Video:** Return to the main menu. Show the saved session under Continue Where You Left, then resume it and reveal its existing areas and collected state.

**Voice-over:**

> Presets, item states, and spatial placements are stored locally. When I continue later, PackSpace restores the session instead of asking me to place every area again.

### 1:38–1:57 — Companion website and code sharing

**Video:** Cut to the website. Create a short categorized list, save it, and clearly show the generated six-character code.

**Voice-over:**

> Lists can also be prepared on the companion website. A friend creates categories and items, saves the list to the Vercel backend, and shares one short six-character pack code.

### 1:57–2:10 — Import into Specs

**Video:** Return to Lens Studio. Open Import Pack Code, type the code, import it, and show the saved preset in the menu.

**Voice-over:**

> In Specs, I enter that code once. The Lens validates and downloads the preset, then saves it locally so it is ready for spatial placement.

### 2:10–2:20 — CLAD and closing

**Video:** Finish on a wide view of multiple outlined areas and the checklist. Optionally overlay “Built with CLAD + Lens Studio.”

**Voice-over:**

> CLAD powered the build, debugging, and preview-verification workflow—from Specs UI and hand interaction to persistence, networking, and spatial placement. PackSpace turns the room itself into a practical packing tool.

## ElevenLabs delivery notes

- Voice: warm, clear, confident; avoid an advertising tone.
- Pace: approximately 135 words per minute.
- Stability: medium-high.
- Insert a short pause after “Snap Specs,” “different height,” “stored locally,” and “six-character pack code.”
